## What Priceline's hotel deals actually look like right now

Priceline's headline hotel discounts come from three places, and they work differently.

**Express Deals** are the deepest cut: up to 60% off select hotels, with one catch — Priceline doesn't tell you which hotel you've booked until after you confirm. These bookings are prepaid, non-refundable, non-changeable, and non-transferable. Priceline's old "Name Your Own Price" bidding tool is gone; Express Deals is the surviving opaque-booking product.

**Pricebreakers** are a milder version of the same idea. You see three similarly priced, well-known hotels in the same area, and Priceline assigns you one of them after booking. Select hotels run up to 50% off, no coupon code needed.

**The VIP program** is free to join and stacks on everything else. Base members get up to 10% off hotels and rental cars with no minimum spend, plus up to 50% off hotels when adding a room to an existing trip. Complete 2 trips in a calendar year for VIP Blue, 5 for Gold, 25 for Platinum — each tier adds Express Deal coupons (up to 10% at Platinum) and better service. VIP members also get Priceline's Best Price Guarantee: find a lower price within 24 hours of booking and Priceline refunds the difference. On Express Deals specifically, that becomes 200% of the difference if you find a cheaper price before midnight on the day of travel. The guarantee excludes loyalty-program member rates, even ones anyone can join for free.

Two smaller things worth knowing. Priceline's app has exclusive deals the website doesn't show, including Tonight Only Deals for same-night stays. And flight-plus-hotel bundles save an average of $240 per person according to Priceline's own figures — treat that as marketing math, but bundles do price lower than booking the pieces separately. Priceline now displays hotel prices with fees included, and search results let you toggle taxes into the total, which matters when you're comparing against other sites that quote pre-tax rates.

## Is booking Priceline hotels through a portal worth it compared to booking direct?

Usually yes on price, with one trade-off you should make consciously. Third-party bookings through OTAs like Priceline generally don't earn hotel loyalty points, and hotels typically don't extend elite-tier benefits — room upgrades, lounge access, breakfast credits — to those reservations. If you're chasing Hilton Honors Gold perks or Marriott nights toward status, book direct.

The price gap has to cover that. In one documented comparison, a 3-night refundable stay in a deluxe king suite at the Conrad New York Downtown priced about $72 cheaper on Priceline than on Hilton's own site. Seventy-two dollars buys a lot of forgone points. On Express Deals the math gets even more lopsided — no points, non-refundable, but discounts up to 60% mean the room itself is often cheaper than any points-earning rate.

Where it flips: short stays at chain hotels where elite status is doing the work, or refundable-rate bookings you genuinely might cancel. Priceline's flexible rates exist, but read each hotel's cancellation terms before paying — they vary property by property.
